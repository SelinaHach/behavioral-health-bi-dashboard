What the dashboard answers

Of patients discharged from substance use treatment in 2023, how many completed their treatment plan, where completion is low, and how early patients leave. One row of data = one treatment episode discharge from SAMHSA's TEDS-D 2023 public file (48 states, DC and Puerto Rico; Delaware, Montana and West Virginia did not report). TEDS counts episodes, not unique people, and mainly covers publicly funded programs.

The five views

  • 1 Scorecard. Headline KPIs, how discharges end, and the national rate under three scopes.
  • 2 States. Completion by state against the scope rate, with peer-range and reporting-review flags. Hover for dropout and transfer rates.
  • 3 Service setting. Completed, dropped out and transferred for the 8 settings at discharge (detox, residential, outpatient).
  • 4 Length of stay. Completion and dropout rate by stay band, and the cumulative share of dropouts gone by each band.
  • 5 Who completes. Completion gap to the scope rate by primary substance, age band, sex and race.

Filters

  • Jurisdictions. All reporting · Exclude reporting-review states (AZ, KY, LA, MD, NC) · Exclude Arizona. Applies to every view.
  • Census region and State. Narrow every view to one region or one state. The States view keeps all states in the region so you can compare.
  • Setting group (view 4). Detox, residential, intensive outpatient or outpatient.
  • Breakdown (view 5). Switches the patient group shown.

Read it correctly

  • Start with the scope. Arizona reports 190,499 discharges at 99.2% completion. The national rate is 42.6% with it, 34.2% without it, and 41.3% without all five review states.
  • Kentucky and North Carolina code close to 90% of discharges as transfers, so their low completion is a coding pattern first.
  • Unknown substance and missing race groups complete at high rates mainly because most of those records come from Arizona.
  • Act on week one. 44.4% of dropouts leave within 30 days and 24.8% within 7 days.

Data quality

Twelve SQL checks run before the views refresh: row count (1,474,025) and column count (76) tie to the SAMHSA codebook, no duplicate CASEID, no blanks in key columns, all discharges in 2023, valid codes, 152 codebook counts reconciled, and no rows lost on the way to the fact table. All 12 pass (dq.check_results).
